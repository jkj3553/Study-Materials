8 Marks1(a) Differentiate between: (i) Waterfall model and Spiral model, (ii) User requirements and system requirements, (iii) Software Engineering and Computer Science

(i) Waterfall Model vs. Spiral Model

ParameterWaterfall ModelSpiral Model
Execution FlowLinear and sequential — each phase must be completed before the next begins.Iterative and cyclical — the project loops through phases multiple times.
Risk ManagementRisk-blind; risks are often discovered too late, during integration.Risk-driven; an entire quadrant of every spiral loop is dedicated to risk assessment and prototyping.
Requirement ChangesVery difficult and costly to accommodate once requirements are frozen.Highly adaptable; changes are absorbed easily in the next spiral iteration.

(ii) User Requirements vs. System Requirements

ParameterUser RequirementsSystem Requirements
Target AudienceClient managers, end-users and business stakeholders.Software developers, system architects and testers.
Level of DetailHigh-level, abstract statements describing what services the system should provide.Highly detailed, structured technical specifications of functions, interfaces and constraints.
FormatPlain, unstructured natural language with simple diagrams.Structured natural-language forms, mathematical specifications or system models.

(iii) Software Engineering vs. Computer Science

ParameterSoftware EngineeringComputer Science
Core FocusPracticalities of developing, delivering and maintaining useful software within schedule and budget.Underlying theories, algorithms and mathematical fundamentals of computing.
OutputHigh-quality, reliable and scalable software products.Mathematical proofs, new algorithms and computational theories.

6 Marks1(b) The ACM and IEEE have cooperated to produce a joint code of ethics and professional practice. Discuss the principles software engineers must adhere to in order to protect the welfare of society.

Software engineers must adhere to eight principles defined by the ACM/IEEE-CS joint task force to protect societal welfare:

  1. Public: Act consistently with the public interest.
  2. Client and Employer: Act in the best interests of the client and employer, while remaining consistent with the public interest.
  3. Product: Ensure that software products and related modifications meet the highest professional standards possible.
  4. Judgment: Maintain integrity and independence in professional judgment.
  5. Management: Engineering managers and leaders shall promote an ethical approach to managing software development and maintenance.
  6. Profession: Advance the integrity and reputation of the profession, consistent with the public interest.
  7. Colleagues: Be fair to and supportive of engineering colleagues.
  8. Self: Participate in lifelong learning and promote an ethical approach to the practice of the profession.

6 Marks1(c) Illustrate the two approaches used in evolutionary development. Analyze the advantages and disadvantages of the evolutionary approach.

Two Fundamental Approaches

  1. Exploratory Development: Works collaboratively with the customer to explore requirements and gradually deliver a final system. Development starts with the well-understood parts and evolves by adding new features as requested.
  2. Throwaway Prototyping: Aims strictly at understanding the customer's poorly defined requirements. The prototype experiments with the unclear requirements and is discarded once the actual system is built through a structured process.

Advantages

  • Customer Satisfaction: The system better meets the customer's immediate needs because they are involved continuously and see intermediate deliverables.
  • Adaptability: Changes in user requirements are accommodated rapidly without derailing the schedule.

Disadvantages

  • Lack of Process Visibility: Managers lack the regular, formal documentation available in Waterfall to measure concrete progress.
  • Poor System Structure: Continual, rapid changes tend to corrupt the software architecture, producing "spaghetti code" that is expensive to maintain.

6 Marks2(a) Three situations faced by a software engineer are given below. Identify the challenge each scenario poses and justify your answer.

i) The application supports a payment option using a third-party payment gateway, accessed through web services.

Trust Challenge (Heterogeneity)

The engineer must integrate external, remote software they did not write. They face the challenge of establishing trust in this third-party service — ensuring it is secure, reliable, and will not compromise user financial data.

ii) The software has to be installed on machines running different operating systems and hardware setups.

Heterogeneity Challenge

Software increasingly needs to run as distributed systems across networks that include very different hardware processors and operating systems. Ensuring cross-platform compatibility is a core heterogeneity issue.

iii) The project is being developed using a process that is consuming a lot of time, extending the project period.

Delivery Challenge

Traditional, large-scale software processes are too slow for modern business needs. The engineer must reduce delivery time — perhaps by shifting to Agile — without compromising system quality.

8 Marks2(b) Consider an insulin pump system with a component that computes insulin dosage based on the patient's sugar levels. Design a system requirements specification for this component using structured natural language.

Requirement Specification: Insulin Dosage Computation Component

  • Function: Compute Insulin Dose.
  • Description: Computes the required units of insulin to deliver, based on the patient's current blood-sugar reading and the previous historical reading.
  • Inputs: Current_Sugar_Reading (integer), Previous_Sugar_Reading (integer).
  • Source: Blood glucose sensor hardware module.
  • Outputs: Insulin_Dose (integer, representing required micro-units).
  • Destination: Insulin pump actuator module.
  • Action:
    1. Compare Current_Sugar_Reading to safe baseline limits (e.g. 80–120 mg/dL).
    2. If Current_Sugar_Reading is below 80 (hypoglycemia), set Insulin_Dose to 0 and trigger a medical alarm.
    3. If Current_Sugar_Reading is above 120 (hyperglycemia), compute the rate of change between current and previous readings, and calculate the dose needed to smoothly return the patient to baseline.
  • Requires: The sensor must have been calibrated successfully within the last 24 hours.
  • Pre-condition: Current_Sugar_Reading must be a valid, non-null integer.
  • Post-condition: The calculated Insulin_Dose is pushed to the actuator buffer and logged in the patient's internal history database.
  • Side-effects: None.

6 Marks2(c) The requirements below need to be identified as functional or non-functional. Justify each.

i) The website's home page should load in 30 seconds and have well-defined frames.

Non-Functional Requirement It does not describe what the system does, but how well it does it — the 30-second constraint is a measurable performance/speed metric.

ii) Users must be able to send messages from their accounts.

Functional Requirement It describes a specific service or feature that the system must provide directly to the end-user.

iii) Every user must be authenticated through their username and password.

Functional Requirement While security overall is a non-functional concern, the specific implementation of a login service that verifies credentials against a database is an explicit functional process developers must code.

iv) Users should be given a virtual keyboard during password entry.

Non-Functional Requirement This is an architectural and security constraint dictating how the login interface must be implemented (to prevent keylogging), rather than a new user goal.

6 Marks3(a) Differentiate between enduring and volatile requirements. Identify two enduring and two volatile requirements for a student information management system responsible for student registration and result generation.

Differentiation

  • Enduring Requirements: Stable requirements derived from the core activity of the organization. They rarely change because the underlying domain — for example, teaching students — remains constant.
  • Volatile Requirements: Requirements that are likely to change during development or after deployment, due to shifts in organizational policy, new laws, or hardware updates.

For a Student Information Management System

Two Enduring Requirements:

  1. The system must securely store each student's name, unique USN (University Serial Number) and date of birth.
  2. The system must be able to generate a final grade transcript at the end of the academic program.

Two Volatile Requirements:

  1. The exact grading rubric mapping percentages to letter grades (e.g. 90+ = S grade), since this policy changes frequently with University circulars.
  2. The web interface layout, or the integration of a specific third-party payment gateway for semester fee payments.

6 Marks4(a) For the applications below, identify the most appropriate control model. Justify your answer.

i) A remote control system used to control a music system.

Interrupt-Driven Control Model (Event-Driven)

A music system spends most of its time idling and cannot predict when a user will press "Volume Up" or "Next Track". The remote control generates an asynchronous hardware interrupt; the system instantly pauses its idle loop, handles the interrupt, and returns to normal — ensuring instant response to human input.

ii) A weather monitoring system that gathers data from sensors such as a rain gauge, temperature sensor and moisture sensor.

Centralized Manager Model (Concurrent Control)

The system requires continuous, parallel data-gathering from multiple independent sensors. A central "manager" process coordinates execution by polling the rain gauge, temperature sensor and moisture sensor concurrently, ensuring data is logged systematically without components colliding.

4 Marks4(c) Discuss the advantages of a shared repository model used for sharing project data among different sub-systems.

  1. Efficient Sharing: A highly efficient way to share large amounts of data; sub-systems do not need to transmit data back and forth to each other.
  2. Centralized Management: Activities such as backups, security restrictions and access control are handled in one central location rather than duplicated across every sub-system.
  3. Independent Operation: Sub-systems do not need to know about the existence of other sub-systems; a new tool can be added and can pull data directly from the repository.

6 Marks5(a) Analyze the series of questions that need to be answered to develop the key project characteristics and an appropriate project plan.

This relies on Barry Boehm's W5HH Principle, a well-known planning outline:

  1. Why is the system being developed? — establishes the business purpose and validation.
  2. What will be done? — identifies the required tasks and project deliverables.
  3. When will it be accomplished? — establishes the milestone timeline and scheduling.
  4. Who is responsible? — defines roles for team members.
  5. Where are they organizationally located? — determines location and communication lines.
  6. How will the job be done, technically and managerially? — sets the process model and architecture.
  7. How much of each resource is needed? — leads to the estimation of effort and cost.

6 Marks5(c) An organization has a burdened labour rate of Rs. 8000 per month and an estimated effort of 36.9 person-months. Given the total function-point count of 340 and the sum of complexity adjustment factors (ΣFi) = 55, calculate: (i) the average productivity of the organization, (ii) the function point estimate, (iii) the cost per function point.

Step 1: Value Adjustment Factor (VAF)

VAF = 0.65 + (0.01 × ΣFi)
VAF = 0.65 + (0.01 × 55) = 0.65 + 0.55 = 1.20

(ii) Function Point Estimate (Total FP)

FP Estimate = Count Total × VAF
FP Estimate = 340 × 1.20 = 408 FP

(i) Average Productivity of the Organization (OAP)

Effort = Total FP ÷ OAP
OAP = Total FP ÷ Effort = 408 ÷ 36.9 ≈ 11.06 FP per person-month

(iii) Cost per Function Point

Cost per FP = Labour Rate ÷ OAP
Cost per FP = 8000 ÷ 11.06 ≈ Rs. 723.32 per FP

8 Marks6(a) An organization has agreed to rapidly develop and deliver a software application to clients. All requirements are expressed as scenarios, the design must be simple, and development must be planned and released in smaller portions. Identify the most suitable development method and discuss the practices involved.

Extreme Programming (XP) — an Agile Method

Practices Involved

  1. Incremental Planning: Requirements are recorded as story cards/scenarios; the client selects the highest-priority cards for the current release.
  2. Small Releases: Software is delivered in small, functional portions rather than waiting months for a large launch.
  3. Simple Design: Just enough design is carried out to meet current requirements — no complex architectural guesswork for unknown future needs.
  4. Test-First Development: Automated test scripts are written before the application code.
  5. Pair Programming: Developers work in pairs at a single workstation for continuous code review and knowledge sharing.
  6. Continuous Integration: As soon as a module is completed, it is integrated into the mainline code and a full test suite is run automatically.

6 Marks6(b) Discuss the advantages of software inspections over software testing. Identify the different roles and responsibilities in the inspection process.

Advantages of Inspections

  1. During standard testing, one error can mask another, forcing the test cycle to restart; inspections can uncover multiple independent errors in a single read-through.
  2. Incomplete versions of the software can be inspected without compiling, whereas testing requires an executable environment.
  3. Inspections improve code knowledge across the team and reinforce domain standards.

Roles in the Inspection Process

  • Author/Owner: The programmer who wrote the code; responsible for fixing bugs found.
  • Inspector/Reviewer: Team members who scrutinize the code line-by-line for logical errors, omissions and standard violations.
  • Reader: Presents/reads the code aloud to the team during the inspection meeting.
  • Scribe: Records all errors and anomalies identified during the session.
  • Chairman/Moderator: Manages the meeting, ensures the process is followed and confirms the author completes rework.

6 Marks6(c) Path testing is a structural testing strategy whose objective is to exercise every independent execution path through a component. For a binary search program, design a path flow graph. Identify the number of independent paths using cyclomatic complexity, and justify.

Control Flow Graph — Binary Search Path Testing

Flow Graph Directions

  1. Draw Node 1 (Initialize Low / High).
  2. Draw Node 2 (While-loop condition: Low ≤ High); if false, path to Node 7 (Return Not Found).
  3. If true, path to Node 3 (Calculate Mid).
  4. Path to Node 4 (If Array[Mid] == Target); if true, path to Node 8 (Return Found).
  5. If false, path to Node 5 (If Array[Mid] < Target).
  6. If true, path to Node 6a (Low = Mid + 1); if false, path to Node 6b (High = Mid − 1).
  7. Connect 6a and 6b back up to Node 2 (While loop).

Cyclomatic Complexity V(G)

V(G) = E − N + 2P (Edges − Nodes + 2)

Counting the predicates — the while loop, the equality check, and the less-than check — gives P = 3.

Alternative: V(G) = number of predicate (decision) nodes + 1
V(G) = 3 + 1 = 4 independent paths

Justification: The objective of path testing is statement coverage. By testing the exact four independent paths derived from cyclomatic complexity, every line of code in the binary search routine is guaranteed to execute at least once, without redundant overlapping tests.

8 Marks7(b) Discuss the set of laws proposed by Lehman with respect to program evolution.

Lehman proposed a set of laws characterizing the dynamics of software evolution for E-type (real-world) systems:

  1. Continuing Change: A system must be continually adapted to changes in its external environment, or it becomes progressively less useful.
  2. Increasing Complexity: As a system evolves, its structure degrades and complexity increases unless active work (refactoring) is done to maintain or simplify it.
  3. Large Program Evolution: Program evolution is a self-regulating process; attributes such as size, time between releases, and reported errors are roughly invariant across releases.
  4. Organizational Stability: Over a program's lifetime, its rate of development is approximately constant and independent of the resources devoted to it.
  5. Conservation of Familiarity: Over the lifetime of a system, the incremental change in each release is approximately constant.
  6. Continuing Growth: The functionality offered by a system must continually increase to maintain user satisfaction.
  7. Declining Quality: System quality will decline unless the system is rigorously modified to reflect changes in its operational environment.

6 Marks7(c) An organization has 10 legacy systems and is planning next quarter's budget, wanting to discard some legacy systems so new systems can be added. Discuss the different clusters that need to be analyzed for discarding legacy systems.

The organization must plot the 10 systems on a matrix assessing two variables — Business Value and System Quality — forming four distinct clusters:

  1. Low Quality, Low Business Value: Technologically fragile systems that no longer support critical business goals.
    Action: Scrap/discard completely to free up budget.
  2. Low Quality, High Business Value: Systems that contribute significantly to the business but are expensive to maintain due to poor code or obsolete hardware.
    Action: Re-engineer to improve quality, or plan a major replacement project.
  3. High Quality, Low Business Value: Technically sound systems that no longer align tightly with business priorities.
    Action: Maintain routine operation with minimal budget, or slowly phase out and replace with cheap COTS (Commercial Off-The-Shelf) software.
  4. High Quality, High Business Value: Critical, well-engineered enterprise systems.
    Action: Leave in operation; continue routine maintenance with standard budget allocation.
